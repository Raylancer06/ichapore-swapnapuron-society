PROJECT ASHIANA

Providing Safe Havens for Birds & Saving Them from Extinction

"Ashiana" means home. We protect vanishing house sparrows and urban birds by installing handcrafted earthen nesting pots, nesting boxes, and freshwater bowls across suburban trees and parks.

forest Why Ashiana

Restoring Lost Nesting Spaces in Modern Concrete Towns

Ashiana means home in Bengali. Our Ashiana project is dedicated to providing safe homes for birds and saving them from extinction.

In our state and country, the population of common house sparrows has drastically plummeted over the past few decades; these once-ubiquitous birds have almost vanished from urban neighborhoods. In earlier times, traditional brick and wooden rafter houses (kori-borga) offered countless safe crevices and cavities where sparrows nested peacefully alongside humans.

As old heritage houses are demolished and glass-concrete apartment blocks rise with flush surfaces and no eaves, sparrows have nowhere left to lay eggs and shelter from rain, heat, and predators.

Through Project Ashiana, we hang natural earthen nest pots (matir handi) and artificial wooden nest boxes on trees in the Ichapur Metal & Steel Factory Park, Barrackpore Station grounds, and neighborhood parks. Over hundreds of installations, we have witnessed with joy that local sparrow populations have rebounded and flourished!

Habitat Revival

Clay nesting pots secured with non-invasive hemp ropes high on tree boughs, supplemented with clean grain feeders and water bowls.

Combining traditional terracotta craftsmanship with wildlife habitat science.

Earthen Nesting Pots

Custom-crafted terracotta handis with calibrated entry holes that keep out predators like crows while welcoming sparrows and finches.

Summer Water & Grain Points

During scorching Bengal summers, our volunteers install shallow water pots and grain feeders so dehydrated birds survive extreme heat.

School & Park Sensitization

We teach school children and park visitors to identify bird calls, avoid harmful pesticides, and place water pots on their house balconies.
